SECTION 10: 
HOURS OF SERVICE
A. DUTY TIME
1. The applicable flight and duty ti me limitations will be those specified 
in Part 117 of the Federal Aviation Regulations including any 
modifications thereto during the life of this Agreement.
2. Duty time will be scheduled for no mo re than fourteen (14) hours per 
duty period; however, a scheduled duty period may exceed this 
limitation with the concurrence of the Scheduling Committee.
3. A flight originating in one calendar day and extending into the next 
calendar day will be considered to have terminated in the first 
calendar day, if it terminates no later than 0200 hours local time of 
the second day.
4. In the event a pilot is scheduled for six (6) consecutive calendar days 
of duty, the pilot will receive one (1) calendar day free from all duty 
before being assigned additional duty. However, a pilot who bids for 
open time flying or who trip trades with open time or with another pilot 
will not be denied such open time or trip trade if such change in the 
pilot’s schedule results in a twenty-four (24) hour break in a seven (7) 
day period, in lieu of a calendar day free from duty.
5. A pilot’s on duty time will co mmence with check-in, which will be 
scheduled for forty-five (45) minutes prior to scheduled flight 
departure. At a layover station, the pilots on duty time may be 
reduced to less than forty-five (45) minutes prior to scheduled flight 
departure, but in no event less than twenty (20) minutes prior to 
scheduled flight departure. When the first flight of a pilot's duty period 
is scheduled to be a deadhead leg, this time will be thirty (30) minutes 
prior to scheduled flight departure.
6. Pilots will be released from duty (check-out time) fifteen (15) minutes 
after block in of a flight (thirty (30) minutes if required to clear 
customs), unless required to reposition an aircraft, in which case, the 
check-out time will be extended as necessary to complete the 
repositioning.
7. The Company recognizes the need to allow pilots to address their 
nutritional requirements during duty periods.

11-21 Q: I am a reserve pilot trying to pick up Open Time on my days off. 
Are there any limits on the times for which OT can start or stop on 
my days off?
A: Crew Scheduling will not award open time which requires a Sign 
in time that begins prior to 1000 (domicile time) on your first day 
off following duty or finishes with a release time after 1800 
(domicile time) on your last day off immediately preceding duty 
unless your schedule has been finalized prior to the awarding of 
the OT. Additionally, such OT must provide a 30-hour break in a 
168-hour period.
11-22 Q: I would like to bid for an open trip sequence which includes an 
outbound leg at the end of this month, with no return leg to the 
domicile in the next month. Can I bid for and be awarded this 
sequence?
A: Yes. You may bid for the sequence, and it will be awarded to you 
in accordance with the regular contractual provisions for bidding 
OT. However, due to a SABRE function, additional legs may be 
added to the end of the trip sequence in the subsequent month, 
and you may be required to fly those legs following the normal 
month-to-month schedule conflict resolution. 
UNRESTRICTED OT LOA QUESTION:
11-23 Q: When can the Company exercise the provision covered by 
Paragraph B.2.b.v. of the Unrestricted OT LOA?
A: Only if the flight that would cause the junior man or extension 
of a lineholder is immediately available. 
11-24 Q: When can a pilot submit a manual OT request for the following 
contractual month?
A: Pilots may submit manual open time (OT) requests immediately 
after the 48 hour OT window closes and may continue to do so 
until the automated OT request system reopens 48 hours later. 
Once the automated system reopens, all FULL OT requests (in 
domicile) must be submitted through the automated system per 
section 11.H.2. Open time requests in accordance with section 
11.H.3-4 may still be submitted manually.
